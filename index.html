RURALEX

Welcome to RURALEX, a three-year research project exploring how knowledge about the environment is changing in rural parts of Europe, and how this shapes the social, economic, and ecological challenges faced by local communities.

About

Changing knowledges

Across Europe, rural regions are undergoing major transformations. Population decline, urban migration, and the loss of traditional agricultural practices have disrupted long-standing relationships between people and their environments. This is leading to many forms of ecological know-how transforming, fading, and being generated. Our research spans diverse sites, from the Spanish Pyrenees to southeastern England, from the Bulgarian Danube basin to the northern cost of Norway. Using long-term ethnographic and archival methods, we document how environmental knowledge evolves across generations and landscapes. We also develop innovative tools to capture stories, sounds, and images that reflect how people relate to their environments. We hope to show how better understanding local knowledge can help address Europe’s pressing ecological and social challenges — and why the humanities are essential to understanding our shared future.

Case studies

Sites of inquiry

Catalan Pyrenees

Northeast Spain

Bureaucratic forest management marginalizes traditional knowledge. As climate change raises the threat of megafires, this project uses ethnographic fieldwork to explore and identify alternative ways to build fire-resilient landscapes and communities.

Read more →

Lower Danube

The Danube Delta, Romania

Socialist Romania and Bulgaria introduced Asian carp as a protein source. It escaped into the Danube, becoming an invasive species. We examine fish reproduction and infrastructure, tracking how neoliberal reforms reshape interspecies relations.

Read more →

Mallorca

Balearic Islands, Spain

Agricultural and environmental practices in Mallorca are changing and so are broader political and social debates. This case study explores resilience and adaptation in labour, trade, and livelihoods, while documenting past agricultural systems and landscapes.

Read more →

Trentino Province

Italy

Abandonment of traditional agriculture, depopulation, and rewilding have turned the landscape into a site of political contention. These transformations spark conflict as local actors negotiate shifting land use and identities.

Read more →

Matsalu National Park

Estonia

Reed beds and flooded meadows along the Kasari River support major Arctic bird migration and foster artisanal innovations. Biodiversity conservation, cultural tradition, and economic viability are negotiated, revealing tensions and adaptations in managing this distinctive landscape and its livelihoods.

Read more →

Rewilding in Sussex

Southern UK

At Knepp, grazing and beaver reintroduction are shaping new wetlands, raising questions about how social values guide future licensing. At Railway Land Wildlife Trust, Lewes, chalk stream restoration created biodiverse wetlands and fosters ecocentric cultures.

Read more →

Sør-Varanger

Northern Norway and Sápmi

Using the spread of invasive pink salmon as a focal point, the case study foregrounds curatorial practice, local art history and educational initiatives, as a way of activating local knowledge of human–environment relations.

Read more →

Bitterfeld-Wolfen

Eastern Germany

Around Bitterfeld-Wolfen in eastern Germany, restored lakes, forests, and recreation landscapes mask a recent history of coal mining and pollution. The project addresses emerging land-use conflicts, particularly around wind farms and solar parks, and their contemporary socio-political significance.

August 16, 2026 · Call for Abstracts · Stefan Dorondel

Applications open: European Society for Environmental History Conference

Our panel 'Desirable Strangers: Historical, Anthropological, and Scientific Perspectives on Species Translocation', convened for the 14th European Society for Environmental History Conference (Salzburg, 7-10 September 2027), invites papers examining the planned movement of species across territories from the modern period to the present. Focusing on translocation as a socio-environmental process, the panel explores its political, economic, scientific, and ecological dimensions. Contributions are welcomed from environmental history, anthropology, STS, conservation, biology, and related fields, particularly those addressing infrastructures, knowledge systems, political economy, ecological outcomes, and interdisciplinary methods. Abstract deadline: 1 October 2026.

June 26, 2026 · Letter · Lucy Sabin

Nature recovery in the air: Community walking as curatorial practice

Now published in TURBA: The Journal for Global Practices in Live Arts Curation, this letter by RURALEX postdoc Lucy Sabin reflects on her fieldwork in Sussex. Artists and writers lead community walks at Lewes Railway Land Nature Reserve, facilitating collective attunement to changing relations between land, bodies, memory, and imagination. Nature recovery, Sabin illustrates, has intangible and atmospheric dimensions.

June 26, 2026 · Interview · Camila del Mármol

The rural cost of the green transition #POLLEN2026

Camila del Mármol, Professor of Social Anthropology at the University of Barcelona (UB) and Esteve Corbera, Professor of Ecological Economics at the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona (UAB) discuss the realities of the green transition for rural communities. It's easy to support the green transition from a distance, but the reality on the ground is much more complicated. In rural Catalonia, a quiet transformation is happening: fields that used to be for farming and pastures are quickly being turned into massive solar and wind infrastructures.

June 22, 2026 · Meeting Report · Lucy Sabin

La libre évolution: negotiating ecological freedom at UNESCO, Paris

Similar to rewilding, la libre évolution is about allowing ecological processes to unfold without continuous human control. RURALEX member Lucy Sabin explains the nature recovery strategy, reporting on the third symposium organised by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) at UNESCO headquarters in Paris. Lucy explores how 'instruments of governance' (here: measurement, law, and mapping) are shaping la libre évolution, its current debates and future possibilities. This article is published open access in peer-reviewed, ecocentric journal The Ecological Citizen.

May 7, 2026 · Poster · Stefan Ottens

Popularising natural sciences in Soviet Estonia

Estonian Naturalists’ Society (Eesti Looduseuurijate Selts) was founded in 1853 and over time became the biggest scientific society in Estonia. Postgraduate researcher Stefan Ottens presented his ongoing research on the topic at the European Citizen Science Association (ECSA) Conference in 2026. "Trustees were a diverse group of people" writes Ottens "from carpenters to car drivers, teachers to high school students, who were united by one ideal: the love for nature".
